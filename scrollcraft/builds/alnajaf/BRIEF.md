# BRIEF: Al-Najaf Specialized Laboratory

**Self-authored under explicit creative delegation.**

The owner answered the structural questions directly and then delegated the
story, the art direction, and the choice of what to film: "the page is like
telling a story but what story or what video will you make choose the best in
your opinion surprise me make something i can really remember and something
that you will be proud of". They pointed at the previous site
(https://alnajaflab.com/) strictly as a source of facts: "DONT TAKE ANY
INSPIRATION FROM ITS DESIGN, its just for you to collect information about the
lab".

Everything below is marked as one of three things:

- **Owner, verbatim.** Their words, unchanged.
- **Evidence.** A fact taken from the lab's own current site.
- **Authored.** A decision I made under the delegation above. The owner can
  overrule any of these.

## The eight topics (Step 0)

### 1. Vibe, and references

Owner, verbatim: "very professional just as a 10000 page", "responsive like all
the buttons and things", "in 3D shape or something like that", "something i can
really remember and something that you will be proud of".

Authored: the vibe in five words is **precise, still, deep, certain, quiet**.

References, from outside the web:

- The opening minutes of *Fantastic Voyage* as they would be shot today by a
  medical-documentary VFX house: a real body interior, photographic, unhurried.
- The Eames film *Powers of Ten*: one continuous move across scales, the
  camera never cutting, each new scale arriving as the last one recedes.
- A signed pathology report on heavy paper: the object this lab actually
  produces, and the thing a patient carries out of the building.

### 2. The scroll journey, section by section

Owner: not stated. Delegated.

Authored, and it is one journey rather than sections because the owner chose a
continuous world:

1. A drop of blood enters a collection tube. The visitor's own moment, macro,
   still. This is what happened to them this morning.
2. The camera enters the tube. Plasma, red cells drifting past at the scale of
   planets. Curiosity.
3. The density climbs. Thousands of cells, faster, busier. The difficulty of
   finding one true signal in all of that, named plainly.
4. One cell fills the frame. The membrane passes over the lens. Near dark. Faint
   chromatin. Nothing is said. **Authored silence.**
5. The helix resolves out of the dark and one base pair lights, the only
   saturated colour on the page so far. The peak.
6. The signal becomes light travelling up through instrument optics. The named
   machines and the named doctors, at the exact point in the story where they
   do their work.
7. The light lands on a signed report on a dark desk. The lab's mark settles as
   a seal. The page stops here and holds, with the real ways to reach the lab.

### 3. The energy curve

Owner: not stated. Delegated.

Authored: a worldflight holds one pace by rule (worldflight.md §7c), so energy
is carried by density and light rather than by speed. Calm at the drop, rising
through the plasma to its loudest at the crowd, then a hard drop to silence at
the membrane, then the peak, then a steady descent to calm at the report. The
loudest act and the quietest act are adjacent on purpose.

### 4. Feeling, stage by stage, and the one moment

Owner: not stated. Delegated.

The feeling curve is its own section below. The one moment they should
remember: **the dark inside the cell breaks and a single letter of DNA lights
up green under their hand.**

### 5. One thing no site they have seen does

Owner: not stated. Delegated.

Authored: **the report writes itself as you travel.** A lab requisition sits at
the edge of the screen from the first frame, empty. Every stage of the descent
fills a line of it: sample received, cells examined, nucleus reached, sequence
read, reviewed by a named consultant. The visitor chooses which test family
they need on the way up, and arrives at the end holding a completed report that
is also their actual booking, pre-filled, ready to send to the lab on WhatsApp.
This is the signature move. It is bespoke page code, not a kit device.

### 6. Aesthetic range

Owner, chosen from the menu: **Premium-minimal.**

The skill notes premium-minimal is "only when asked for". It was asked for.
The trap it warns about (dark, one accent, and nothing decided) is answered by
the accent being a specific, meaningful colour: the fluorescence green of a
positive signal in a real-time PCR instrument, which is literally the colour in
which this lab reads an answer. It appears only where a result is being read.

### 7. One unbroken world, or distinct scenes

Owner, chosen from the menu: **One continuous world.**

This forces the **Continuous world** grammar, which requires worldflight mode.
Not negotiable and not blended with anything else.

### 8. Assets they already have

Owner, verbatim: "i can supply you the images but the videos we need to generate
them and i want it to be free".

Evidence: the previous site carries no photography at all. Its only image is a
254-pixel JPEG of the logo: a red crescent around a green ring, a red-and-blue
DNA double helix, the word "Laboratory", the Arabic "النجف", and a faint
silhouette of the Najaf shrine behind.

Authored: every clip in the world is rendered locally in three.js and encoded
with the skill's own encoder. No paid generation. The subject matter (things
smaller than the eye can see) cannot be photographed by the owner in any case,
so rendering is the honest representation rather than a fallback. The rendered
world is held to a photographic standard by its preamble (below), not to a
diagram or clay look. Owner photographs, when they arrive, go to the places
where real people and real rooms carry trust: the team and the instruments.

Requested from the owner, non-blocking: a vector or high-resolution logo, and
photographs of the doctors, the instruments, and the building.

## The feeling curve

Written before any leg existed. Emotion first, cause second.

```
1  Recognition   a drop of blood slides down glass, macro, held; it is theirs
2  Curiosity     the camera enters the tube; red cells drift past like planets
3  Unease        thousands of cells, faster, denser; which one is telling the truth
4  Held breath   one membrane passes over the lens; near dark; nothing said
5  Awe           the helix resolves and one base pair lights green under the hand
6  Confidence    the signal rises as light through the named instruments
7  Calm          the light lands on a signed report; the seal settles; it holds
```

No two adjacent legs share a feeling. The loudest (3) sits directly before the
quietest (4), which is what gives the peak (5) something to arrive from.

## Grammar, and why the other seven lost

**Continuous world.** Chosen by the owner at question 7, which settles it. For
the record, the others against this brief:

- Filmic one-shot: built from pinned acts, and the owner asked for one unbroken
  world; the act-based attempt at that has already been judged "awful".
- Chaptered editorial: hard cuts between chapters are the opposite of one world.
- Live surface: the lab has no software product to operate.
- Typographic poster: the brand's asset is not a sentence, it is a place inside
  the body no visitor has seen.
- Gallery / catalog: the visitor's question is "can I believe you", not "what
  are the options"; the seven services are real but they are not the argument.
- Split stage: there is no two-sided comparison here.
- Rhythmic cutlist: a pulse is wrong for a diagnosis.

Nav, hero and close follow from the grammar: no section navigation (there are
no sections), a route rail that is the requisition itself, a hero that is the
first frame of the flight, and a close that is the flight landing.

## The score

A worldflight has legs rather than acts and holds one pace. Rate is weight
divided by film seconds and is held constant at about 0.217 vh per second,
the floor worldflight.md §7c recommends for a world you fly through.

| Leg | Film | Weight | Feeling | What the camera does | Copy window |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Drop | 6s | 1.30 | Recognition | Macro on an EDTA tube; a drop runs down the inner wall; slow push until the glass fills the frame | hero, greet |
| 2 Plasma | 6s | 1.30 | Curiosity | Inside: translucent amber, red cells drifting past, forward glide | plateau |
| 3 Crowd | 6s | 1.30 | Unease | Same scene, density and speed climb, frame busier | plateau |
| 4 Membrane | 5s | 1.08 | Held breath | Approach one cell, pass through its wall into near dark | none (authored silence) |
| 5 Letter | 10s | 2.17 | Awe | Helix resolves from dark; slow orbit and push to one base pair which lights green | plateau, late |
| 6 Reading | 6s | 1.30 | Confidence | The green signal rises as light through glass optics; amplification curves; a flow cell lights cluster by cluster | plateau |
| 7 Report | 6s | 1.30 | Calm | Light lands on a report sheet on a dark desk; the seal settles | finale, hold |

Sum of weights 9.75; track 10.75 viewport-heights.

Seams are matched by construction: every leg is rendered from one scene
system, and each join lands on a low-detail frame (glass fill, continuous
plasma, membrane fill, dark, light bloom, light bloom) so the one-sided
crossfade has nothing to hide.

## Build decisions, recorded as they were made

**Copy anchors, desktop.** Hero bottom-left. Plasma top-left. Crowd
bottom-right. Peak centred, held to 18 characters wide. Reading top-left.
Finale a three-column composition: copy, booking form, requisition with the
seal beneath it. Four anchors across the page, never the same one twice in a
row.

**The requisition sits top-right, under the bar.** It started bottom-left and
covered the hero's buttons on a 900px-tall screen; there is not room in one
column for a three-line display headline, a lede, two buttons, and a seven-line
panel. Top-right keeps it visible through the whole flight, leaves the hero
alone, and lets the finale reserve its column so the three end up side by side.
On phones it is a bottom bar that expands on tap, and on desktop the same
button collapses it, so the control is never focusable-but-inert.

**Hero composition, re-rendered.** The first render centred the tube, and the
contrast pass caught the headline at 2.45:1 on the frame where the glass
highlight passed under it. Rather than a heavier scrim, the landscape camera now
looks past the tube to the left, so the tube stands in the right third and the
copy has dark ground. The label was turned to the far side of the tube on both
orientations. Portrait keeps the tube centred, since its copy sits low over the
blood.

**Peak copy window widened** from 0.64 to 0.655 through 0.742 with a fast
ramp-out, so the line holds until the letter's leg ends instead of flashing on
two samples.

**Authored silence is longer than leg 4.** Nothing is said from the crowd's
question (0.385) until the letter lights (0.655): the membrane, the dark, and
the helix emerging from it. That is the quiet in front of the peak, and it is
deliberate. The requisition still writes "Nucleus reached" and "Sequence read"
inside it, so the panel is the only thing that moves while the world does.

**Scrim plates are siblings.** One plate per copy block in the copy layer,
mirroring the block's engine-written opacity each frame, so the contrast pass
measures what the reader sees. Lead, top, trail, centre, and finale shapes.

**Faded copy leaves the tab order.** A block under 5% opacity gets
`visibility: hidden`, so the hero's buttons and the finale's form are only
reachable when they can be seen. Keyboard focus landing in the finale early
flies the page to the end.

**No JavaScript** lays the story out as a plain document: the first poster, the
copy in order, the contacts, and the booking links. The form is `method="post"`
with its submit hidden, so a scripted-form fallback can never put a name and
phone number into a URL.

**No WebGL** leaves the live layer hidden and the posters and copy carry the
composition. Reduced motion never fetches a clip and never starts the live
layer.

**The verification browser.** Playwright's Chromium cannot decode H.264, so
every clip reads as frozen there. All passes run on Google Chrome. The engine
instance is aliased to `window.__sc` in the mount line because the upstream
assertion script reads it there.

**Fonts are self-hosted.** Archivo, Geist, Geist Mono, and IBM Plex Sans Arabic
as woff2 under `fonts/`. Chrome's requests to Google Fonts were reset from this
sandbox, and a visitor in Najaf should not depend on a third party for the type
either.
